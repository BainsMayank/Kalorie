## 1. To ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition

**Subject:** Permission to use IFCT 2017 values and the 2020 RDA/EAR tables in a free nutrition app

Dear Sir/Madam,

I am building **Kalorie**, a free calorie and nutrition tracking app for Android and iOS, made
for Indian food. It has no ads, no paid features and no in-app purchases, and it does not
sell or share anyone's data. I started it for my family and friends and would like to release
it publicly on the Google Play Store and the App Store.

I would like your permission to use two NIN publications in the app:

1. **Indian Food Composition Tables 2017** (Longvah T, Ananthan R, Bhaskarachary K, Venkaiah K).
   The app holds the nutrient values per 100 g of 514 raw foods from IFCT 2017, so that people
   can look up and log foods like atta, dal, rice, vegetables and fruits.
2. **Short Report of Nutrient Requirements for Indians — RDA and EAR 2020.** The app uses the
   RDA, EAR, adequate intake and tolerable upper limit values to suggest daily nutrient targets.

How they are used:

- Only the numbers are used, typed or read into the app's database. Neither document, nor any
  page, table layout or text from them, is copied into or shipped with the app.
- The app's source code is public on GitHub, and its built food database (which holds these
  values) is part of it. I would like the permission to cover that too; if you would rather
  the values were not in a public repository, I will keep the database file private.
- Both are credited by name on the app's About screen: "© ICMR–National Institute of
  Nutrition, Hyderabad", with the full citations. I am happy to use whatever credit wording
  you prefer.
- No changes are presented as NIN's. Where the app corrects an obvious slip (for example, fish
  vitamin B6 values that appear 1,000× too high), this is noted in the app's source notes, and
  I would be glad to report these to you if useful.

Could you please let me know whether NIN permits this use, and how you would like to be
credited? If a formal application or fee is needed, please tell me the process.

The app's privacy policy: [link to privacy page]
The app's source code: https://github.com/BainsMayank/Kalorie

Thank you for making these tables available — they are the backbone of how Indians understand
their food.

Yours faithfully,
Mayank Bains

## 2. To Anuvaad Solutions (Indian Nutrient Databank)

**Subject:** Permission to use INDB 2024.11 in a free, ad-free nutrition app

Dear Anuvaad team,

I am building **Kalorie**, a free calorie and nutrition tracking app for Android and iOS, made
for Indian food. It has no ads, no paid features and no in-app purchases, and it does not sell
or share anyone's data. I started it for my family and friends and would like to release it
publicly on the Google Play Store and the App Store.

The **Indian Nutrient Databank (INDB 2024.11)** is what makes the app useful for Indian
home cooking: it uses the per-100 g nutrient values of its 1,014 dishes, and the recipe
ingredient lists, so that people can log dishes like dal, sabzi and khichdi and adjust the oil
in them. I could not find a licence for the database itself (on anuvaad.org.in or in the
GitHub repository), so I would like to ask your permission directly.

How it is used:

- The values are read into the app's own food database. The spreadsheet itself is not shipped
  with the app.
- The app's source code is public on GitHub, and its built food database (which holds these
  values) is part of it. I would like the permission to cover that too; if you would rather
  the values were not in a public repository, I will keep the database file private.
- INDB is credited on the app's About screen, citing: Vijayakumar A, Dubasi HB, Awasthi A,
  Jaacks LM. *Development of an Indian Food Composition Database.* Current Developments in
  Nutrition 8 (2024) 103790. I am happy to use whatever credit wording or link you prefer.
- A few rows that look like data-entry slips (for example *Black forest gateau* at 219 mg
  vitamin C per 100 g) are left out of the app's suggestions; I would be glad to send you the
  list if it helps.

Could you please confirm whether Kalorie may use INDB in this way, and under which licence (for
example CC BY 4.0, like the paper)?

The app's privacy policy: [link to privacy page]
The app's source code: https://github.com/BainsMayank/Kalorie

Thank you for building and sharing INDB.

Best regards,
Mayank Bains
